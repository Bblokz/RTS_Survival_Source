# Oder-Warthe mission: radio direction finding (design draft, working title "Funkpeilung")

Status: concept only, nothing built. Started 2026-11-16. Lore reference: `Source/DesignTexts/Lore.txt`.
Related: [BerlinMission.md](BerlinMission.md) (the mission before), [PripyatMission.md](PripyatMission.md) (later, further
east), [OreMountainsMission.md](OreMountainsMission.md).
Every item marked **(open)** is undecided. Everything marked **proposal** is my suggestion and still needs the owner's OK.

## One-line pitch

A Shield command post is hidden somewhere in an old German bunker line, under fog. The player captures direction-finder
stations. Each one draws a bearing on the minimap. Two bearings narrow down the area, and three pinpoint it. The enemy's
radio is loudest when it gives orders, so the player baits it into talking.

Identity: **detective work on the minimap.** The HQ cannot be seen. It can only be heard.

## Layout sketch (picked 2026-11-16): "Corner Ridge over the Lakes"

The mission logic and step-by-step flow on this layout are in [OderWartheMissionFlow.md](OderWartheMissionFlow.md).
Render copies: [layout](OderWartheMission_layout_C.png), [example bearings](OderWartheMission_bearings_C.png).

The owner picked this layout from six MapDesigner sketches (three without a base, then three with one). The owner's
reason for the change: there are already many missions where the player cannot build, and by now the player knows how
to build, so **this is a base-building mission**. The sketch replaces the "Map character" section below where they
differ. Everything in it is still open to change.

- **Spec:** `MapDesigner/OderWarthe/df2_c.layout.json`. It is generated by
  `MapDesigner/OderWarthe/generators/gen_df2_c.py`, so change the generator, not the JSON.
- **Regenerate** from `MapDesigner/`: `python OderWarthe/generators/gen_df2_c.py`, then
  `python md.py all OderWarthe/df2_c.layout.json --out OderWarthe/out2_c` and
  `python OderWarthe/df_overlay.py OderWarthe/df2_c.layout.json --out OderWarthe/out2_c`.
  The out folder is not tracked.
- **`df_overlay.py`** is a mission-local script, not part of the map tool. It draws example bearing wedges (+-15 degrees
  from the first two stations, +-5 degrees from the first three), and checks every bunker group as the HQ.
- **Size:** 700 m east-west by 620 m north-south.
- **Base ridge:** a flat headland ridge, 18 m high, in the south-west corner. The build circle is 85 m, and the flat top
  runs on into the corner so the base can grow. A crag band under the rim means every unit, infantry too, must use one
  of three graded ramps (12-14%):
  - the north ramp leads to the Packlitz dam;
  - the prow ramp leads to the island causeway over the Mittelsee;
  - the east ramp leads to the Kalau causeway.
- **Lake basins:** five finger lakes radiate from the Mittelsee and cut the rest of the map into four basins. Basins are
  linked only by dams, causeways, a ford and infantry boardwalks. Outposts (the bait) hold every crossing.
- **One station and one resource site per basin**, so taking a causeway gives an expansion and a bearing at once:
  - north basin: DF 2 and a metal dump;
  - south-east basin: DF 3 and the large radixite field;
  - middle basin: DF 4 and the rich metal;
  - north-east basin: DF 5 and a radixite vein (the hardest to reach).

  DF 1 is at the foot of the north ramp. A small radixite seep and a small metal node are on the ridge top.
- **Five identical bunker groups** sit on promontories and necks. Each has a tunnel mouth that launches attack waves,
  all with the same weight, so the waves do not reveal the HQ. Every wave crosses a causeway before it reaches a ramp
  (north 40%, prow 20%, east 40%). **Defending the base is baiting:** each attack order is the loudest burst and narrows
  every held wedge.
- **Bearing check:** for every bunker group as the HQ, DF 1 + DF 2 leave 2 groups and DF 1-3 leave only the HQ.
- **Known limits of the sketch:**
  - The "near" resources are 300-400 m from the base by path, because the ramps wind.
  - DF 5 gives the best crossing angle for three of the five groups only.
  - The map tool cannot model dragon's teeth (a barrier that stops vehicles only), so they are drawn as wire.
  - The "establish the base" objective is a stand-in: hold the ridge through the first wave. No build objective class
    exists.

## Location (proposal): the Oder-Warthe Bend fortress line, western Poland

The **Festungsfront Oder-Warthe-Bogen** (Polish: Międzyrzecki Rejon Umocniony), around Meseritz / Międzyrzecz, about
130-150 km east of Berlin. It is a German fortress line built 1934-1944: steel-cupola Panzerwerke on the surface, more
than 30 km of tunnels underneath with an electric narrow-gauge railway (the "Regenwurmlager", the earthworm camp), lines
of dragon's teeth, and a system of lakes, dams and sluices built to flood the ground in front of the line. In real
history the Red Army overran it at the end of January 1945 with almost no fight. (These facts, and the dates in this
section and the alternatives table, are from memory: **verify** before any of them go into game text.)

### Why it fits

1. **It comes straight after Berlin.** In the Berlin doc the Shield response force arrives **from the east by rail and
   by road** (checked in BerlinMission.md). This line is directly east of Berlin, so it is where that force is commanded from.
2. **It gives a reason for the mission.** **Proposal:** when the resistance gets the Zoo tower radio working in Berlin,
   it also hears heavy Shield command traffic. One bearing from Berlin points east into the Oder-Warthe Bend, but one
   bearing only gives a direction, not a place. Hence this mission. The traffic is heavy *because of* the Berlin
   broadcast: the CSD thought the Germans were dead and is now hunting them.
3. **A hidden HQ is believable here.** The line has many identical bunker groups, so it gives natural **decoy sites**:
   the player can see five bunker groups but does not know which one is the command post. The real command post is
   **underground**, which is why recon from the air does not find it (see "Stopping brute-force scouting").
4. **It foreshadows the Burrowers** (lore 2.5: survivors in "subterranean military bunkers"), the same way the Ore
   Mountains mine tunnels do.
5. **It is Act 1 ground.** The area is not a firebreak (working assumption, as in the Ore Mountains doc). The Phytophage
   went through: dead pine forest, peat bogs, and lakes choked with purple algae. In the tunnel mouths we can use the
   Suffocator visuals from lore 2.7 (creeping mats hanging from tunnel ceilings, veins wrapped round old crates).
6. **It has a real Soviet history.** After the war the Soviet army kept a secret base beside the line at Kęszyca Leśna
   until the 1990s (**verify** before using it in text). The CSD reusing the site fits the same way Wismut fits the Ore
   Mountains.
7. **Historical irony for the briefing.** The bunkers face **east**. The resistance comes from the **west**, behind the
   guns, and the Soviets have had to add west-facing positions.

## Place in the campaign

- **After Berlin, before Pripyat** (proposal).
  - After Berlin, because the Zoo tower radio is what makes direction finding possible (see above).
  - Before Pripyat, because Pripyat unlocks the ME410. The player should not have strong air scouting yet.
  - Order: Intro, Ore Mountains, Berlin, **Oder-Warthe**, ..., Pripyat (about 1,000 km further east).
- Tone: **a hunt.** After the desperate raid in Berlin, the resistance takes the fight to the voice that hunted it.

### The generated world map

The campaign map is generated from anchor points. What exists in code (checked in
`WorldCampaign/CampaignGeneration/GenerationRules/MissionPlacementRules.h`):

- `FPerMissionRules` has a tier, hop limits from the player HQ, and **`OverridePlacementAnchorCandidates`**: a list of
  hand-picked anchors. This is how to pin the mission to western Poland. Place some anchors in the Lubusz area and list
  them there.
- The world map has country meshes (`FWorldCountryOccupationActorPair` in `WorldCountryOccupationRegulator.h`). I did not
  check which countries the map has, or that it is a real map of Europe.
- The player HQ is generated, so pinned anchors can fall outside a hop window. With the override list, the override hop
  fields (`OverrideMin/MaxHopsFromPlayerHQ`) apply; keep them loose or the mission may not place.
- `FMapObjectAdjacencyRequirement` can require nearby companion objects within N hops.

Proposal:

- **Tier 2**, 2-4 hops from the player HQ.
- **Adjacency:** at least one enemy **Barracks or Factory** within 2 hops (the command post commands something nearby).
- **New `EMapMission` entry** (name **(open)**, for example `RadioDirectionFinding`). Note: Berlin and the Ore Mountains
  have no `EMapMission` entry yet either. How the story missions map onto the enum is **(open)**.
- **Campaign reward (proposal):** the captured signals archive reveals enemy map items within N hops in the world fog
  (the `WorldFow` folder exists; whether it can reveal a radius is unchecked), and nearby enemy items lose strength for a
  few turns because their command post is gone (a field in the existing strength-reason system; unchecked).

## Map character

*Written before the layout was picked. Where it differs from the "Layout sketch" section above, the sketch wins: for
example the start is now a ridge base in the south-west corner, not the west edge.*

- **Size (proposal): about 700 x 600 m.** That is between the built Ore Mountains map (570 x 570 m) and the Berlin sketch
  (1000 x 520 m). The 850 m Ore Mountains sketch was found too large. The map must be wide enough that crossing angles
  matter (see "Geometry is the puzzle").
- **West edge:** the player's start, on the old road from Frankfurt (Oder) and the Obra river crossing.
- **Middle to east:** the fortress line runs roughly north to south. **Five bunker groups** (the candidate sites), each a
  cluster of steel cupolas, a tunnel mouth, dragon's teeth and some Soviet additions.
- **Lakes and dams** break up the map and limit armour routes. **(open)** whether a sluice can be opened to flood a
  sector as a tactic (the real line was built for this).
- **Dragon's teeth lines:** vehicles are blocked, infantry can cross. This gives infantry a job and guides armour.
- **Dead pine forest** with peat-bog patches and smouldering spots. Algae lakes in purple.
- **Unexplored-fog reveal on** (`AMissionManager::bRevealUnexploredFog`). The player can see the terrain and the bunker
  groups as landscape from the start, so the minimap reads like a map to plot on. Enemy units and buildings stay hidden:
  `FowComp.cpp` hides its owner when it is not seen (checked). Unchecked: that every Soviet building class carries a
  `FowComp`.

## Core mechanic: bearings

### The direction-finder stations

- **Five stations** (proposal) spread round the map, not in a line. Each is a small capturable site: a hut with a
  rotating loop aerial or a four-mast Adcock array, a generator and a flag.
- **Capture** like the existing `ASupplyPoint` (the flag rises while the player holds the area). Whether it can share
  the supply point's capture code is unchecked.
- Each station has a **small garrison**. Stations further east are better defended.
- **The enemy can retake a station** (proposal) once it notices the direction finding (see Phase 3). A lost station keeps
  its last bearing on the minimap, but in grey, and the bearing stops narrowing.

### What a station shows

- A held station draws a **bearing wedge** on the minimap and the full map view: two thin lines from the station towards
  the HQ, with a light fill between them.
- The wedge starts **wide** (proposal: 20 degrees each side) and **narrows each time the station hears the HQ transmit**
  (minimum about 2 degrees). The centre of the wedge starts with a random error that shrinks with the width, so an early
  wedge can be a little off.
- **Two wedges** overlap in an area. The minimap highlights the overlap. With wide wedges it covers one or two bunker
  groups.
- **Three wedges** make a small triangle (navigators call it the "cocked hat"). When the triangle is small enough and
  lies on a bunker group, it is a **fix** (Phase 4).

### Transmissions: why baiting works

The HQ transmits in bursts. Each burst a held station hears narrows its wedge.

| Burst | When | Wedge narrows by (proposal) |
|---|---|---|
| Routine traffic | Every 60-90 s | A little (x 0.9) |
| Reply to a contact report | 10-20 s after the player attacks an **outpost** or **any** bunker group | Medium (x 0.7) |
| Attack order | When the HQ launches an attack wave | A lot (x 0.5) |

- **Outposts** are the small Soviet positions between the bunker groups (checkpoints, patrol posts, station garrisons).
  They are the bait.
- **The five bunker groups all answer the same way.** Attacking any of them (the real HQ or not) gives the same reply
  burst, so poking each group and watching the gauge does not give the answer away.
- **Baiting:** poke an outpost and pull back, or let an attack wave come and fight it. Both make the HQ talk.
- **Feedback:** a burst plays Morse on a radio channel, and a signal-strength needle jumps on a small gauge in the
  mission UI. The WWII gauge kit (`Frostyle`) could supply the gauge (unchecked).
- Attack waves can come from the existing `AEnemyWaveSpawner` (its `OnUnitsSpawned` event can fire the "attack order"
  burst; checked in memory notes, not in code).

### Geometry is the puzzle

Bearings from stations close together are almost parallel and overlap in a long, useless strip. Bearings that cross at
a good angle make a small area. **Which stations the player takes matters.** The two western stations are easy but give
a poor fix on their own. A northern and a southern station cross well. The eastern station, behind the line, gives the
best fix and is the hardest to take.

## Mission flow (proposal)

1. **Briefing.** The advisor explains the Berlin bearing: the voice that sent the column after us in Berlin is
   somewhere in these bunkers. The start force is mobile: **(open)** whether there is a small base (HQ, barracks,
   refinery), as in Berlin (small force) or the Ore Mountains (base).
2. **First station (teaching).** A station near the start. When it is captured, the first wide wedge appears. Soon after,
   a scripted probing attack comes in, and the wedge visibly snaps narrower. The advisor names the rule: **"When they
   attack, they talk."**
3. **Spread out.** The player takes more stations. Two wedges show the first overlap area. The advisor: "Two bearings,
   a rough area. We need a third."
4. **They notice.** After the first two-wedge overlap, the HQ realises it is being direction-found:
   - it sends small forces to retake held stations;
   - routine traffic gets less frequent, so the player has to bait more.
5. **Fix.** Three bearings meet on one bunker group. Reveal it: vision bubbles over the site (`AGlobalAbilityVisionBubble`
   exists), destroy markers and minimap icons on the targets, and a short camera tour. `USecureBaseMission` already does
   reveal, tour, markers and a destroyed count (see GerIntroMission.md), so this phase can probably reuse it.
6. **Assault.** The command post is a fortified bunker group with a tunnel mouth. Targets (proposal): the command bunker
   entrance, the transmitter array and the tunnel railway entrance. These are camouflaged and **only appear at the fix**
   (or when the player's units reach the group's tunnel mouth); before that the group looks like the other four. When the
   command post falls, enemy waves stop and the remaining garrisons no longer reinforce each other.
7. **Signals archive (optional).** An infantry squad enters the destroyed command bunker to take the archive. This is the
   lore payoff and the campaign reward (below).

### Decoy transmitter (proposal, Hard and above)

- A Soviet radio truck drives the roads and transmits **on the same schedule as the HQ**, from the start of the mission.
  Every held station shows **two** wedges from its first burst, drawn the same way. Both start wide and both narrow
  with each burst, so neither is obviously the fake.
- The truck moves between bursts, so **its wedges swing** to a new centre each burst, while the HQ's wedges stay put.
- Two decoy wedges can still cross and make a convincing area. A third does not pass through it, while the HQ's three
  wedges meet in one point on a bunker group. This is where "two lines narrow, three pinpoint" earns its rule: **two
  lines can lie, three cannot.**
- Destroying the radio truck removes the decoy wedges. Optional side hunt.
- **(open)** whether a moving source is readable enough in play, or whether a fixed decoy (whose fix lands in empty
  forest, away from every bunker group) is clearer.

### Relocation (proposal, Brutal and Ironman)

- After a fix, if the command post is not destroyed within N minutes, the commander leaves by the **tunnel railway** to
  another bunker group.
- The old bearings go grey, and the player must find the HQ again with the stations they hold. This is faster the second
  time, because the stations are already taken.

### Wrong guesses

- Assaulting a bunker group that is not the HQ is allowed. It costs time and units, because every group has a garrison
  and the same west-facing defences.
- There is no special penalty and no special signal: the reply burst is the same as for any group (see the burst table),
  so a wrong guess teaches nothing for free.

## Stopping brute-force scouting

The mission fails as a puzzle if the player can just look. Proposal:

- **The command post is underground.** On the surface all five groups carry **the same kit**: the same cupolas, tunnel
  mouth, aerial mast, garrison size and unit types. Recon flight, Spies and plain scouting only show five identical
  garrisons. The HQ's own buildings and its signals units stay underground and only appear at the fix, or when the
  player's units reach its tunnel mouth.
- **Spies infiltration** (`GA_GerSpiesInfiltration`, reveals 6 random hidden enemies): it shows garrisons, which every
  group has, so it gives no answer. It stays useful for spotting the decoy radio truck and for station fights.
- **Global ability tier cap:** `PlayerGlobalAbilitySettings.MaxAllowedTier` can hide high-tier abilities on this map.
  It caps by tier, not by ability, so blocking one ability would need new code. With the identical-groups rule above no
  ability needs blocking. Recon flight's tier is unchecked.
- **Cost of guessing:** see "Wrong guesses".

## Win and loss

- **Win:** the command post targets are destroyed.
- **Loss (proposal):** the player loses all units (and the HQ, if there is a base). No hard timer. Relocation (hard
  difficulties) is the time pressure.
- **(open)** whether the signals archive is required or a bonus.

## Lore payoff

- **Act 1 clue (proposal):** the bunker still holds **1946 Red Army papers** from before the CSD existed (the Directorate
  formed in 1948-1975): quarantine orders for "Sector Lebus", orders to burn grain stores, "Fitofag" contamination of
  wheat. They hint that the purple death was aimed at **crops**, not people, without explaining it. This feeds Act 1
  ("it was a famine weapon").
- **The CSD's reaction to Berlin:** the current (1976) Shield logs show that the Berlin broadcast reached them, and that Shield has started
  hunting German cells. This raises the stakes for the rest of the campaign.
- Speaking characters: the German advisor (GermanAnnouncer portrait), and intercepted Russian radio lines from Shield
  operators with English subtitles, as in the intro mission. The old characters section of the lore (Korovin, Projekt
  24) is not used: it is not revised for the CSD setting.
