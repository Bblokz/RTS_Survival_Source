2 Lore Introduction

Operation Svarog & why

The Central Soviet Directorate (CSD) is the last superpower standing. The Soviets used nukes to win
WW2, so why do they no longer use them? That question, and many others, is what the player should be
asking after the intro cinematic.

The resistance faction is German and is caught between the eastern and western powers. It makes
perfect sense that they are not up to date with everything that happened. The player should feel the
same way at the start of the game: they only know fragments, and most of those are wrong.

2.0 High level timeline

October 1944 (shown in the intro cinematic)
  A month after the first V2 launch at England, Soviet spies get access to German V2 technology.

January 1945 - The Fall of the Reich
  The Soviets successfully launch a V2-like rocket carrying their nuke at Berlin.

May 1945 - The Manhattan Catastrophe
  Fearing retaliation, the Soviets launch a coordinated, long-range atomic strike on the American
  Manhattan Project facilities. The US and UK are crippled, their leadership scattered, and World
  War II effectively ends with absolute Soviet hegemony.

August 1945 - The Purple Death is unleashed
  With their militaries shattered, a rogue faction of surviving Allied scientists and intelligence
  officers enacts a doomsday revenge protocol. They release the "Phytophage" over the Ukrainian and
  Siberian breadbaskets, timed with the autumn harvest, to starve the Soviet juggernaut. It
  immediately mutates out of control.

1945 - 1946 - The Choking Year
  Things happen very quickly. The Phytophage mutates ridiculously fast. It consumes not only the
  wheat it was designed for but other biological material as well. Millions die directly from
  exposure to what is now called the green death. The Phytophage jumps continents, destroys most of
  the food on Earth and starves millions more.

1946 - 1947 - Operation Svarog
  As a desperate act to stop the Phytophage spreading further east, the Soviets execute Operation
  Svarog: they nuke several parts of their own land, creating abiotic firebreaks, and then retreat
  behind the Ural Shield. A massive, mostly unrecorded war raged across European soil, likely
  between advancing Red Army units and hundreds of thousands of scattered Western divisions.

1948 - 1975 - A Revolution
  Something changed in the Soviet dictatorship. The absolute rule of a single dictator was violently
  dismantled (see The Directorial Power) and replaced by the CSD.

1976 - The player's era (30 years later)
  For thirty years the CSD has locked itself behind the iron walls of the Ural Shield, with only
  scattered Soviet outposts throughout western Europe. They believe Svarog erased the Germans.
  They are wrong.

2.1 The Phytophage

Known as the Phytophage, the pathogen did not just kill crops. It mutated, jumping to all cellulose
and organic matter. It turned forests into toxic, self-combusting peat bogs and choked rivers with
black (purple), suffocating algae. It was fast, airborne, and threatened to starve the entire planet
within years.

In 1976 the purple Phytophage is still active in some areas.

2.2 Radixite Birth

When the CSD enacted Operation Svarog, the V2 nukes did not just incinerate the Phytophage. They
caused a catastrophic chemical reaction. The intense atomic heat fused the bio-engineered Phytophage
matter with irradiated minerals, which created what is now called Radixite.

- This is why Radixite seems alive: part of the bio-weapon is still in there. (In game it already
  grows and makes sound.)
- Because of its organic origin it acts like a sponge for radiation.
- Because of the Phytophage it grows roots like plants. (Already in the game.)
- Maturation: it takes decades for the Radixite veins to grow deep into the crust and boil off the
  oceans and water tables, transforming the world into scorched desert.
- Some water still remains but is clearly contaminated.
- The Radixite heating effect is everywhere.

2.3 Why nukes are no longer used ("The Dead Sky Threshold")

The sky is dark but the ground is boiling.

- The Chain Reaction Risk: Radixite is highly volatile when exposed to atomic fission, which is why
  the CSD no longer uses nukes.
- The Powder Keg Effect: dropping a nuke on the surface is like lighting a fuse that runs straight to
  the planet's core. The atomic heat travels along the Radixite root system at supersonic speeds.

2.4 The Directorial Power

Why is there not a single dictator? The Svarog Mutiny. Stalin went mad after his daughter died to the
Purple Death, and launched more nukes on the motherland in the hope of wiping it out. (IRL Svetlana
was his only daughter; he was close to her.) A mutiny came and resulted in a coup.

The power is divided into three pillars, the Troika of the CSD:

- Director of Shield (the Weapon): exterior military and perimeter defence. Commands the heavy tanks
  and mechs, artillery arrays, and the shock-troops that guard the glassed Svarog Line.
- Director of Forge (the Engine): oversees the massive subterranean foundries. Their troops wear
  heavy thermal shielding and cryo-suits, and wield Radixite-powered weaponry.
- Director of Harvest (the Sustenance): controls the food supply.

2.5 How Nomads Emerged

The survivors adapted to the heated landscape. Remnants of former greatness (twisted, rusted steel
skeletons of buildings, scattered vehicle parts, and World War II artefacts) are strewn across the
wasteland. Scattered groups of German and Western Allies survivors live as nomads, scavenging for
essential resources, particularly Radixite crystals.

The Burrowers
Survival by going deep. When the firebreaks were detonated, remnants of local populations, scattered
military units, and miners retreated into the vast underground networks of Western Europe and
western Russia (metro systems, deep salt mines, subterranean military bunkers).

  The "Gandy-Dancers" (Armored Combat Engineers): named after old-time American railroad track crews,
  these are the shock-troops of excavation. They do not just clear the path; they build the war zone.

2.6 What the player thinks vs. what they discover

Several things are very unclear to the player at the start:
- Why would the Soviets nuke part of their own land? (The player is led to believe it was due to a
  second war with the Western coalition.)
- Why did the Soviets stop using nukes?
- How was Radixite formed exactly?
- Why are there transition zones of purple mold-like glue to Radixite?
- What weapon did the Western coalition unleash against the Soviet nuclear empire?
- Aren't the Soviets supposed to be a dictatorship? Why is it now acting like a board?

Act 1: The Purple Death
  Thinks: the Allies dropped a generic bio-weapon that killed millions on contact.
  Discovers: it was not meant to kill people directly. It was a desperate famine weapon (the
  Phytophage) that mutated out of control.
  - Environmental clues: ruined Soviet agricultural sectors (massive, rusted silo complexes). The
    ground is choked with fossilized, blackened, twisted roots instead of normal ruins.
  - The Drop Pods: a crashed, 30-year-old Allied stealth bomber or payload pod. Inside are rusty
    canisters labelled "Project Demeter: Wheat Defoliant - For Agricultural Targets Only."
  - Audio logs / intel: old, desperate radio recordings of Allied scientists crying, realizing the
    pathogen has jumped from cellulose to human tissue, apologizing for dooming the world.

Act 2: The Firebreak (the truth of Operation Svarog)
  Thinks: the Soviets nuked their own territory in a fit of paranoid madness, or to scorch the earth
  against the Western coalition.
  Discovers: the nukes were surgical tools. They created a "Glass Wall" to stop the Phytophage
  reaching the Ural Mountains.
  - The Svarog Line: a perfectly straight, 50-mile-wide canyon of smooth, green, vitrified glass.
  - Abandoned command posts: inside ruined CSD watchtowers along the glass line, tactical maps show
    the relentless eastward spread of the "Green Death" stopping exactly where the nukes were dropped.
  - The narrative shift: the CSD did not nuke their own cities out of cruelty. They sacrificed
    millions of their own people to build an abiotic wall to save the rest.

Act 3: The Beating Heart (the truth of Radixite)
  Thinks: Radixite is a weird, volatile rock caused by radiation that powers their weapons.
  Discovers: Radixite is a living, subterranean root network. It is the fossilized, mutated remains
  of the Phytophage, feeding on atomic energy.
  - Mining outposts: at a CSD Radixite mining facility, instead of pickaxes the CSD uses extreme
    cryo-tech to "sedate" the crystals before harvesting.
  - Interactive environments: when the player fires heavy weapons or uses Radixite-powered gear near
    natural veins, the crystals glow brighter, pulse, and physically expand toward the heat source.

Act 4: The Kola Incident (why nukes are banned)
  Thinks: the Soviets stopped using nukes because they ran out, or because the atmosphere could not
  take it.
  Discovers: dropping a nuke will ignite the Radixite root network and blow the planet's crust apart
  from the inside.

2.7 Visual design of the Phytophage

Very distinctive from the Radixite crystal: a purple, wet and moldy look.
- Specular gloss map (wetness shader).
- Very slow, sine-wave driven WPO coupled with a panning normal map, for slow movement and maybe a
  glow effect depending on the normal map.
- Fresnel effect mapped to the edges of the mesh geometry, combined with a highly contrasting
  roughness map. Deep crevices and thick bulges are hyper-glossy and wet; as the mesh wraps around
  corners the Fresnel scatters light to simulate millions of tiny, dry fungal hairs.

Mesh design: The Suffocator
- The Creeping Mats: a thick, sagging blanket. When it fills a hallway it covers and rounds out the
  sharp corners of the walls. It hangs from the ceiling in heavy, purple, dripping shapes like melted
  wax or thick, cooling tar.
- Necrotic Pustules: dark purple pods in tight clusters in room corners or around pipes. Some look
  shriveled and dead; others are still swollen and tight.
- The Veins: thick, raised tubes connecting the mats that pulse gently like human blood vessels.
  They actively search for organic things, wrapping tightly around old wooden supply crates and the
  bones of 1940s soldiers, completely swallowing them.

The Radixite Transition (visual storytelling): in levels near the nuclear impact craters, the player
finds a room covered in purple Tar-Blight. Closer to a massive blast hole in the wall, the purple mesh
physically shatters and spikes, transitioning seamlessly into the jagged, glowing orange crystals of
Radixite. It is the visual proof that the bio-weapon and the fuel source are the same entity.

2.9 Website timeline (archival text)

"Archival Notice: About what follows, a lot is as of yet unknown. Thirty years of static, ash, and,
well, the nukes, left huge blackouts in our history. What is presented here is compiled from the
limited data available."

The Stolen Blueprints - October 1944
  Soviet spies infiltrate classified testing facilities, securing advanced German V2 rocket
  blueprints just a month after they first strike across the English Channel. While it seemed that
  atomic weapons were still far away for the Western allies, the Soviets are ahead of the race and
  use the V2 technology to build the world's first nuclear missile.

The Fall of the Reich - January 1945
  The Soviets successfully launch a modified V2 rocket. Its target: Berlin. The resulting atomic
  detonation instantly vaporises the city, breaking what was left of the German war machine in a
  single strike.

The Manhattan Preemption - May 1945
  Because of failing diplomacy and fearing the Western Coalition is developing a nuclear arsenal of
  their own, the Soviets strike across the ocean. Manhattan Project facilities are wiped out.
  Western leadership is scattered, and World War II seems to end in absolute Soviet hegemony...

The Purple Death - August 1945
  Unable to accept defeat, a rogue faction of the shattered Western powers enacts some kind of
  desperate doomsday protocol.
  Archival Notice: Apart from the mold-like purple growth found all over Europe not much is known
  about this presumably genocidal weapon.

Operation Svarog - 1946 - 1947
  A massive, mostly unrecorded war raged across European soil, likely between advancing Red Army
  units and hundreds of thousands of scattered Western divisions. For reasons unknown, the Soviets
  began nuking vast amounts of territory they already controlled, which we later came to know as
  Operation Svarog. This ended with most Soviet troops and civilians retreating eastward behind the
  iron gates of the Ural Shield.

A Revolution - 1948 - 1975
  Following the blasts something changed in the Soviet Union's dictatorship. The absolute rule of a
  single dictator, the defining pillar of the Soviet state, was violently dismantled. In their place
  stood The Central Soviet Directorate (CSD), a technocratic military oligarchy governed by a supreme
  council of three: The Troika.
  CSD Troika = Director of Shield (The Weapon) + Director of Harvest (The Sustenance)
               + Director of the Forge (The Engine)

The Resistance - 1976
  For thirty years, while the CSD Troika locked themselves behind the iron walls of the Ural Shield,
  there have been only scattered Soviet outposts throughout western Europe. They believe Svarog
  erased us, but they are wrong. And now the time has come to take what is ours!

characters:
(Carried over from the previous version of this file; not yet revised for the CSD / Troika setting.)
🔶 1. The Hazmat Trooper — “The Canary”

Role: Early-game presence, frontline scout, first taste of Soviet power.
Personality: Loyal but shaken; indoctrinated but still human.

Backstory
Originally a cleanup soldier assigned to Radixite extraction sites.
Saw entire units collapse from exposure; his suit is both his armor and his prison.
Taught to fear the outside world more than the Soviets themselves.
Believes deeply that he’s serving “the last real government on Earth.”

Traits

Cautious, paranoid, always scanning the horizon.
Constantly checking his suit seals, muttering radiation readings under his breath.
Obeys orders instantly — fear of punishment is immense.

Narrative Purpose
Introduces players to the aesthetic and danger of Soviet-controlled zones.
Provides small hints that the Soviets are hiding something massive:
“Strange readings again… probably just those… machines they’re building.”

🔶 2. The Cyborg Soldier — “Unit-7 / The Iron Warden”

Role: Mid-game antagonist, symbol of Soviet transhumanism.
Personality: Cold, mission-focused, stripped of identity.

Backstory
Former elite soldier who volunteered (or was coerced) into the Soviet cybernetics program.
His limbs and organs were replaced after Radixite exposure nearly killed him.
Now controlled by a neural inhibitor — but fragments of his old self sometimes leak through.

Traits
Precise movements, unnatural posture.
Voice has slight metallic distortion.
Sometimes hesitates when facing civilians or resistance fighters — memory echoes.

Narrative Seeds
He references Projekt 24 without naming it:
“Our enhancements are nothing compared to what comes next.”
“The Commander says the machines will make us obsolete. I hope that is true.”

Purpose
Represents the moral decay of Soviet experimentation.
Builds tension toward the bigger reveal of Projekt 24.

🔶 3. The Cortex Commander — “Marshal Korovin”

Role: Main antagonist, leader of the Soviet Remnant.
Personality: Calculated, authoritarian, visionary but blinded by ideology.

Backstory
One of the original Soviet military scientists.
Exposed to radiation early in the war—saved by experimental cybernetic implants.
His enhanced cortex allows him to process battlefield data in real time.
Believes the Soviets saved the world by being the only surviving organized nation.
Sees all nomads as destabilizing threats.

Traits
Calm, unnervingly confident.
Speaks slowly but with absolute authority.
Half his skull is reinforced plate; cybernetic eye tracks everything.

Motivation
He believes in a future where humanity survives through order and machinery, not chaos.
Link to Projekt 24
He authorized it.

One of his lines foreshadows it:
“Steel does not tire. Steel does not doubt. Steel will rebuild what flesh destroyed.”
“When Projekt 24 marches… balance will return to the world.”
